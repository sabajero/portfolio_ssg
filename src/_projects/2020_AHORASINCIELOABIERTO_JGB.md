"Now, Without an Open Sky" is an artistic research project aimed at capturing the emotions, thoughts, and experiences of 10 young university students during the confinement brought about by the Covid-19 pandemic in Cali. Each illustration was born from dialogue with the participants, focusing on what they deemed significant to be depicted. The culmination of this project are ten watercolor paintings, each offering insights into the city and the confinement experience from an intimate perspective. This project is hosted within the virtual exhibition at ahorasincieloabierto.tumblr.com.

At the end, the watercolors were also a cartography of the city of Cali, since each image was accompanied by a short story or comment extracted from the focus groups with the participants.

***This is a winning project of the 'United for Life' call, supported by the Burough of Culture of Cali, 2020.***

## Gallery

**Isabella, La Luisa path, Comuna 19**
"At first, the emotional part was like a roller coaster... everything was so unexpected. There was nobody around my house when I went out... now I go to the river a lot and have discovered several new places."
*Watercolor on Hanemüller paper, October 2020, 24cm x 30cm.*

**Camilo, Brisas de los Álamos**
"Nobody in the neighborhood cared a damn. In Brisas, all the food joints are operating as usual, and everyone goes out like nothing's happening. Where my girlfriend lives, though, it's more extreme, with parties and nightlife. At first, I enjoyed it because I wanted a break... but now I'm disappointed in Cali."
*Watercolor on Hanemüller paper, October 2020, 24cm x 30cm.*

**David, Barrio Pance**
"Everything has remained very flat because I am not very used to going out frequently… it has hurt me not to see people but in a very minimal way… At work I have felt more relaxed and financially it has been a respite…"
*Watercolor on Hahnemühle paper, October 2020, 24cm x 30cm*

**Joan, Yanaconas streets**
"Despite the harsh consequences of the pandemic, for some it was the perfect opportunity to learn new things... there were falls and I got really bad scrapes but it has been a blast"
*Watercolor on Hahnemühle paper, October 2020, 24cm x 30cm*

**Yeisson, Los Pinos**
"Being locked up didn't impact me much... the tranquility it has brought to my neighborhood is as if in some moments time had stopped... it's a different Cali, I constantly perceive a lack of trust in others. It's a strange community feeling that I'm not used to. The city is like in a coma, the street dwellers have disappeared, I even see the sky differently."

**Jerónimo, Mélendez**

**Juan Pablo, Primero de Mayo**
"It has been a creatively frustrated quarantine, in the sense that the resources I had before to create were at the University… every time I see someone it is much more special, every experience with someone I think is more meaningful. Personally, I have had a character evolution, I began to think a lot about my things, about myself, about how I am as a person, attitudes I had and such, it was a gonorrhea process, it still is…"

## Concept

This project aims to construct images that represent what the city signifies during times of pandemic, particularly for the youth in Cali. The driving force behind the project is the necessity to produce images that significantly contribute to the construction of the visual collective memory of the Covid-19 pandemic, acknowledging it as a disruptive experience where society is both producing and consuming a vast amount of information and imagery. The project is justified in the sense that historically, the city has been represented by artists who, through their works, have portrayed visions, emotions, imaginings, critiques, symbols, and more, thus shaping an idea of the city for the future.

The general objective of the project is to depict the experiences and imaginations of university students in Cali through ten watercolor illustrations to aid in the construction of the visual memory of the pandemic lockdown. The specific objectives include: 1. Identifying the experiences with the city of 10 young individuals across different locations in Cali. 2. Creating 10 illustrations in watercolor techniques based on these individuals' experiences. 3. Digitizing the watercolors and disseminating them through a virtual exhibition.

The route designed to achieve these objectives is divided into three stages: 1. Preparation and Call for Participation. 2. Conceptualization, design, and realization of the illustrations. 3. Online diffusion and awareness. It's essential to highlight that in the creation of the illustrations, project participants are active agents who influence the conceptualization and final outcome of the 10 watercolors, as their feelings and experiences will be reflected in them.

In this sense, the project aligns with the concept of audience development, breaking mental barriers among participants and the audience, such as the notion of the solitary artist creator and the idea that art cannot be created collectively. The beneficiary population includes young men and women aged between 18 and 25, adults with latent demand according to manual classification, university students from all socioeconomic strata in the city of Cali and its surroundings. The final product expected is 10 illustrations that will be digitized for dissemination on social media and also in a virtual exhibition.
