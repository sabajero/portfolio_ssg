*"Your words were still present", Animation, 1 min. 2019.*

"The Strongest Bull" delves into the artist's personal experience of family disappearance, aiming to resonate with broader themes of disappearance through imagery that prompts reflection in viewers. The project serves as a critique of family narratives, archives, memories, media, and societal realities. It seeks to prompt reconsideration of entrenched structures, rather than offering solutions to societal forgetfulness and indifference towards historical memory processes.

The artist's journey to Buga to visit their aunt, Maria Elena Balanta, sister of their missing uncle, Héctor Mario, involved collecting photographs, letters, diplomas, clippings, and objects related to him. Selection of items for the archive was meticulous, focusing on their potential relevance to the theme of disappearance. Initial experiments with the compiled file proved unsuccessful, leading the artist to explore interpretation of images, particularly utilizing the motif of rubble to symbolize disappearance, and evoking the missing uncle's presence through reproductions of his writing. The use of an image depicting a bull bleeding to death in a bullfight serves as a poignant metaphor for the artist's uncle's fate.

## Research and Archive

*Photos taken as part of the project research, 2019*

As part of the research for the project I interviewed my aunt Maria Elena, although more than an interview it was a conversation where she showed me for the first time the family archive of my missing uncle and told me that it was the first time she had seen the photos from her disappearance. Through this process it was possible for me to understand that the generational pain of a forced and violent disappearance promotes the emergence of narratives to deal with grief, these narratives are reflected in memories and invented stories, sometimes even fantasies, which is fine and It is part of the mourning and memory processes. Also part of what I finally wanted to represent at work was my uncle's strong and fast personality, which, according to my family, led him down wrong paths, which to this day generate pain because he was a person with a promising future and with family. Part of dealing with generational grief on my part and to give temporary closure to an incomplete life story was making these animations that symbolically reflect on the forced disappearance in Colombia and the years of violence in the 80's and 90's.
